# A2 · Cultural safety, trust and responsibility review of the v2 plans

Reviewer A2, one of the review swarm. Written Oct 24, 2026, 2:45 PM PT, before any v2 step started (`status.md` still shows v1's `W2+P2`), so every fix below is a plan or data edit, not a rewrite.

Read for this review: `docs/v2/brief.md`, `docs/v2/README.md`, `docs/v2/contracts.md` (sections 2, 6 and 11 closely, plus 3, 4, 5 and 8 to trace events and labels), the plans `S3-catalog`, `B11-embed`, `B14-swarm`, `B15-watch`, `F-communities`, `F-watch`, `F-storefront`, `F-support` and `M-buyer`, and, to trace where guardrails live, `I2-core` T5, `I2-kit`, `I2-art`, `I2-tour`, `B12-search`, `B13-live`, `F-search`, `F-process`, `F-seller-hub`, `M-approvals`, `D-sponsors`, `.claude/rules/mock.md` and v1's `src/mock/catalog.ts` and `people.ts`. Legal and cultural facts were checked on the web. Sources are at the end.

## Verdict: safe with fixes

The plans already take more cultural care than most hackathon builds. Nothing sacred is in the catalog, no one writes in an Indigenous language, Gu embroidery is never called Indigenous, a person approves anything that moves money, publishes or reports, the Steward's quotes must exist word for word, and simulated panels carry a badge. The scripted tour on Gordon's own machine is close to safe. Three gaps are not. First, in live mode the counterfeit watch puts a real web seller on screen, and in the video, as a look-alike of a fan drawn for this demo, and its hit rule flags any seller who claims the craft and isn't on Cindia, which by design includes genuine Gu embroiderers. Second, the demo speaks and vouches for real Peoples: community pages are set as the community's own words, Cindia's own exclusions are shown as the community's rules, and verification lines say fictional makers passed checks that never ran, with nothing on a public URL to say it's all fictional. Third, nothing checks what Claude writes (training pairs, drafts, tags, replies) for the banned items, clichés and invented meanings the data tests look for, and the Steward's answer isn't bound to its quote. Every fix is wording, data, a label or a small server check. None touches the architecture.

## What each lens sees

### 1. Cultural-safety advisor

- **What lands well.** A named banned list (contracts section 2). `nativeName` unset and no Indigenous-language text (section 11). Gu always heritage (glossary, `F-communities.md` acceptance 3). The maker's share shown before paying, and projects get the full contribution (figure 13). The tour keeps Indigenous pieces to search results (d01) and one surprise draw (d02).
- **What doesn't.** The brief says the seven communities are there "so the search, the storefronts and the counterfeit watch have real range". Real Peoples' names, places and crafts become range for a demo whose story is a Shanghai studio. That's acceptable only if every page makes plain that the co-ops are placeholders that speak for no one (findings 2 to 4).
- **What a Diné visitor would feel.** On `/shop/community/com_dine` as planned, she meets a co-op that doesn't exist, speaking as "we" in words an AI wrote, a rule her people never made ("no Yei designs", though Diné weavers have woven Yei rugs for the market for about a hundred years), makers who passed "a check against tribal enrollment" no one ran, and a rug she can buy with a demo card. A small Demo data badge sits beside it all. A Sámi visitor finds a store at `/store/sami-duodji`, the name of the Sámi certification mark. A Chinese judge reads 苗族 next to 原住民. Each of these is fixed by wording and data, not by cutting communities.
- **The Indian Arts and Crafts Act** makes it unlawful to "offer or display for sale, or sell any good ... in a manner that falsely suggests it is Indian produced". A demo isn't a sale, but a public page with fictional "Diné" rugs, a Pay button and a verification line looks like the display the Act describes (findings 2, 7 and 11).
- **CARE principles.** Collective benefit is designed in. Authority to control is where the plans slip: the community's voice, rules and verification are written for it (findings 3 and 4), and synthetic text about real Peoples trains a model that may be published (finding 12). Responsibility and ethics rest on the banned list and the model card, once both are enforced (findings 8 and 12).

### 2. Visa's trust and responsibility judge, and the UX judge

- **Privacy.** The card is masked and never typed (v1 `F-buyer.md` T3), and keys stay on the server. But buyer-side agents receive every order, payout and maker statement, `order_lookup` answers any order ID, live questions go to Claude with no notice, and live steps reach every open screen (finding 14).
- **Transparency.** Live, Simulated, Rule-based and Demo data badges, the agent's name on each reply and the step strip are strong. Weak spots: verification lines (3), "91% similar" (13), "hand-written" test queries (12), invented provenance (18), and "I'll ask the maker" with nothing behind it (9).
- **Security.** Found URLs are text, never links or images (`F-watch.md` acceptance 4), B15 never fetches a found page, and every model answer is typed and ID-checked. Web snippets are untrusted input to Claims and Reporter. Typed outputs and the quote check contain that, and a templated report closes it (13).
- **Responsible use of AI.** The seal, narrow tools and the forbidden-event sweep (`B14-swarm.md` T9) are the best part of the plan. The gap is text, not actions (8 and 9).
- **UX.** Labels risk clutter. One plain line in the top bar (L1) lets every badge stay small. Give each hit a dismiss as easy as the report (13). Never answer a question about one piece with another piece's record (10).

## Catalog content rules for the data-smith

These replace and sharpen the cultural care section of `S3-catalog.md`. Where a rule changes a contract, the orchestrator settles it first.

**Communities**

- **R1.** `kind` is how the community would describe itself. Gu is heritage. Miao becomes heritage too, with `ich_listing` (finding 5). Never write "Indigenous", "Native", 原住民 or 土著 about either.
- **R2.** `place` is a region: Navajo Nation, USA. Aotearoa New Zealand. Sápmi, northern Norway. Nunavut, Canada. Cusco region, Peru. Guizhou, China. Kajiado County, Kenya. Never a town known for a real co-op (Chinchero), and never the name of a real co-op, studio, gallery, trading post or association (Kinngait, Cape Dorset, the CTTC).
- **R3.** `name` describes and doesn't brand: "A Diné weaving co-op". No names in an Indigenous language.
- **R4.** `about` is two plain sentences in the third person about the craft and what the co-op makes. Never "we", "our", "ancestors", "elders", "for generations", "ancient", "timeless" or a hardship story. The file comment says it holds the place of the community's own words.
- **R5.** `protocols` are Cindia's demo exclusions in plain words ("No ceremonial pieces", "No Yei or sandpainting designs"). Never phrase one as the community's own rule or belief. These are the only `Text` fields where banned words may appear.
- **R6.** `verification.note` says what a real launch would use and ends "No real check was done for this demo." For `com_dine`: "In a real launch, makers would be enrolled members of the Navajo Nation or certified by it as Indian artisans, as the US Indian Arts and Crafts Act defines. Cindia would keep only a yes or no, never the record." Don't write "tribal enrollment", so the word scan stays simple.
- **R7.** Never show, name or imply a real authenticity mark for a demo maker: the Igloo Tag, Toi Iho, Sámi Duodji, Sámi Made, or any Nation's own mark. Slugs `sami-duodji` and `chinchero-weavers` become `sami-workshop` and `andes-weavers`.
- **R8.** `languages` is `["zh"]` for Miao, not `hmn`. Never write "Hmong" for the Guizhou co-op.

**People**

- **R9.** Gu and Miao people keep full Chinese names. Indigenous makers and leads get a common English, Spanish or Norwegian given name and a family initial ("Grace N."), with `surnameChar` the initial. Never a full family name, a name in an Indigenous language, or the surname of a famous artist family (for example Ashevak, Ashoona or Pootoogook in Kinngait).

**Pieces**

- **R10.** Titles name the object and its colors or pattern: "Grey and red wool rug". Never a People's name as a product word ("Navajo rug", "Maasai bracelet", "Inuit-style"), and never "tribal", "Aztec", "Inca" or "chief".
- **R11.** `story` is about the piece and the work: what it is, the materials, the hours, one plain detail about colors or size. No family lineage, spirituality, hardship, poverty, "dying art" or "last weaver", and never a claim that a real person made it.
- **R12.** `heritage` is the community's one craft line from the safe facts table below, the same for each of its pieces. Don't copy v1's "Peonies stand for wealth and honor" pattern. v1's twelve Gu lines are mainstream Han Chinese symbolism and stay exactly as they are. No other piece gets a meaning.
- **R13.** `process` steps are physical: prepare the fiber, dye, warp, weave, carve, print, finish. No harvesting protocols, prayers, blessings or ceremonies. Hours still add up.
- **R14.** `motifs` are plain visual words: flower, leaf, bird, fish, butterfly, diamond, zigzag, stripe, step, dot, wave, chevron. Never a named culture-specific motif, and never what one means ("symbolizes", "represents", "protects", "brings luck", 象征, 寓意).
- **R15.** A flower motif goes only where the craft is known for flowers: Miao embroidery, Andean textiles (flowers are a common design element there), an Inuit print of plants. Don't add flowers to a Diné rug, a Māori kete or a Sámi band to make figure 11 work. If three pieces can't qualify honestly, put it under `needs:`.
- **R16.** Materials: no ivory, bone, whalebone, baleen, sealskin or fur. The US Marine Mammal Protection Act bars most buyers from bringing marine mammal parts into the US, so Inuit pieces are stone or paper. No feathers. Write "cream", never "ivory", even as a color, and avoid "feather stitch".
- **R17.** Add to the banned objects in contracts section 2: Miao silver headdresses and festival costumes (such as 百鸟衣), Maasai bridal or life-stage regalia, whirling-log designs, masks, drums, rattles, pipes, prayer or "medicine" items, dreamcatchers, headdresses and totems.
- **R18.** `vibe` words come from one closed list. Never: exotic, tribal, primitive, ethnic, mystical, spiritual, shamanic, sacred, ceremonial, spirit, totem, boho, native-inspired, authentic, ancient, timeless, warrior, princess, chief, powwow, smudge, healing, energy.
- **R19.** In Chinese text, Indigenous craft terms stay in Latin script with their marks and a gloss: "kete（亚麻编织袋）". Peoples take the standard Chinese name with the self-name: 纳瓦霍（Diné）, 毛利（Māori）, 萨米（Sámi）, 因纽特（Inuit）, 克丘亚（Quechua）, 马赛（Maasai）.
- **R20.** Spell Māori, Sámi, Sápmi, Diné, pōtae and whāriki with their marks in every `Text`. Never "Eskimo" or "Lapp".

**Projects, storefronts and listings**

- **R21.** Project text says what the money pays for and how much, plainly. No rescue language ("save a dying tradition").
- **R22.** Visit blocks hold the region and "Visits by arrangement". No street address, phone, email or hours, for every storefront including Gu.
- **R23.** Hero taglines are plain craft lines ("Handwoven wool rugs and sash belts"). No slogans about spirit, ancestors or authenticity.

**Search, the eval set, the watch and the swarm**

- **R24.** `keywordAliases` never map a stereotype word ("tribal", "native") to anything.
- **R25.** The 50 eval queries are "held-out test queries, written separately", never "hand-written", unless Gordon writes them. No banned or stereotype words in them either.
- **R26.** Watch candidates use example.com or example.org and invented sellers, and resemble Gu pieces only. `cft_01` shows its evidence: its title names the studio and Lu Yun, its snippet repeats `pc_01`'s first story sentence, and its claim reads "Says it's hand-stitched by Lu Yun of the Gu Embroidery Studio. She didn't list it there." `cft_02` becomes the claim-only example: it says "hand-stitched Gu embroidery", names no studio, and gets no report. `cft_04` still covers the low score.
- **R27.** Scripted authenticity replies name who attests (L15), and `run_01` answers only for `pc_01`.
- **R28.** One list in code: `src/mock/contentRules.ts` exports `contentRules` with `bannedObjects` (contracts section 2 plus R16 and R17), `stereotypeWords` (R10 and R18), `meaningWords` (R14), `accusingWords` in English and Chinese (finding 13) and `peopleNames` (each People's names in English and Chinese, plus Indigenous, Native, 原住民 and 土著). The data tests scan every v2 `Text` against it, except `protocols` and v1's twelve Gu records. It needs an export line in contracts section 11.
